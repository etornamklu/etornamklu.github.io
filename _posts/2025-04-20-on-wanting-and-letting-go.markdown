What do you do when you're caught between wanting someone so deeply—and wishing you could feel nothing at all for them?

For a while, you were always on my mind. I wanted to talk to you, hear how your day went—to be someone you wanted to share your world with. But eventually, I had to admit something hard: I wanted that closeness to go both ways. I wanted to be asked about *my* day too. To be seen. And deep down, I knew that wasn’t going to happen. I knew you weren’t interested. Not really. But I still held on. I convinced myself that if I was persistent enough, patient enough, good enough—you’d finally see me.

But looking back, I see what that made me. Desperate. Clinging to hope without a foundation. I thought I was being brave by holding on—but now I see how small it made me feel. How much of myself I gave to someone who never asked for it.

This quarter has been tough—mentally, emotionally. A lot of reflection. A lot of rewiring. I used to believe that if I just stayed long enough—if I tried hard enough—I’d get the girl. That effort could somehow compensate for indifference. But that’s not how it works.

I’ve come to understand that relationships—real ones—aren’t built on one person’s will. They’re built on shared desire, shared effort. It’s not just about what *I* want or what *you* want, but whether *we* want the same thing—and whether we’re both willing to show up for it.

I thought I mattered. That belief made me open up, share my world, let someone in. And maybe that’s what hurt the most—realizing that love, no matter how honest or full-hearted, still needs a place to land. And when it doesn’t… you’re left holding something no one ever asked for.

If at any point I pushed too hard or held on too tightly—I’m sorry. That was never my intention.

But even in the pain, there’s clarity.

This experience taught me something I’ll carry with me: love should never be begged for. And feeling deeply isn’t a weakness—it’s proof that I *can* love, fully and freely. That I *have* something worth offering. And one day, I hope to offer it to someone who meets me halfway.

Maybe what’s hard isn’t just letting go—it’s facing the silence that follows. The space where you start again. But I’m learning that space isn’t empty—it’s sacred. It’s where becoming happens.

This isn’t the end of anything.  
It’s just another page in the story.  
Still here. Still becoming. -->
